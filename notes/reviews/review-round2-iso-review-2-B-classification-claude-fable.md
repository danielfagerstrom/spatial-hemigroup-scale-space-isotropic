## 1. Overall assessment and recommendation (for part B)

**Minor revision.** The axioms are stated twice, on operators and on kernels, and the two definitions correspond clause by clause; Lemma 3.3 proves the correspondence completely, including the one non-trivial point, that L¹-continuity of the operators (A7) and pointwise continuity of the transforms (I2) are the same condition. The reduction to the line (Lemmas 3.4, 3.5) is correct. Theorem 3.6 proves exactly what §1 and §4 claim of it: in every dimension the kernels from scale zero of an isotropic family are, up to the relabelling (χ, F) ↦ (cχ, F(c⁻¹·)), the dilations of one rotation-invariant self-decomposable law, Lemma 3.8 realises every admissible pair, Proposition 3.10 identifies the class A_d with the exponents of the isotropic origin data and shows that the exponent of every rotation-invariant self-decomposable law is admissible, and Proposition 3.11 carries the time-causal families across with the three named members, with the cited statement numbers of [10] and [11] correct. I read every proof of this part line by line. I found one error in a printed proof (a dominating function that is not integrable as written, p. 37, trivially repaired), one wrong cross-reference (p. 39), one citation that does not cover the full parameter range it is cited for (p. 19), and one loose headline sentence (p. 29). None of these affects a result. The formal inventory of Appendix B agrees with `lean-axiom-report.txt` for every declaration of this part. The main thing that is improvable is length: the proof of Lemma 3.5's continuity clause (a page) is made redundant by Lemma 3.3(2), and Lemma 3.3's proof could be shortened with a citation.

## 2. The headline claims against what is proved

**The main result as §1 states it** (p. 4, "What the paper contributes"; p. 3, "The answers", first paragraph; abstract, sentence 4). The claim is: the rotation-invariant self-decomposable laws on R^d are exactly the kernels from scale zero of the isotropic scale spaces that the axioms define; in every isotropic family the kernels from scale zero are, up to a relabelling of the scales, the dilations of one such law, which is not the point mass; and every such law other than the point mass gives an isotropic family for every relabelling.

This is what the statements prove, with the following chain, which I verified:

- *Direction "kernels are such laws".* Theorem 3.6 (necessity, proved in the main text p. 16) gives a gauge χ and F ∈ A_d, F ≢ 0, with μ̂_{0,t}(ξ) = e^{−F(χ(t)|ξ|)}, so μ_{0,t} = (χ(t)I)_* μ_{0,t₁} with χ(t₁) = 1, and μ_{0,t₁} is self-decomposable with residuals μ_{s_λ,t₁} (p. 16, last paragraph of the proof). A dilation of a self-decomposable law is self-decomposable, so every μ_{0,t}, t > 0, is one; it is rotation invariant by (I3) and not δ₀ because F ≢ 0 and χ(t) > 0 (this last step is not written but is immediate from (ND)).
- *Direction "every such law occurs".* Given a rotation-invariant self-decomposable μ ≠ δ₀: μ̂ is real (Lemma 3.4(1)), nonvanishing (C4 and infinite divisibility), hence positive, so F(r) := −log μ̂(re₁) is a real function with F(|ξ|) the exponent of μ; Proposition 3.10(1) and (2) make F admissible, so F ∈ A_d, and F ≢ 0; Lemma 3.8 then gives, for every gauge χ, an isotropic family with (3.1), whose kernel μ_{0,χ⁻¹(1)} is μ. This chain is correct, but it is nowhere written out as one statement: the reader has to assemble it from Theorem 3.6, Lemma 3.8 and Proposition 3.10 and supply the step "μ̂ > 0" himself. The citation "(Theorem 3.6 with Lemma 3.8 and Proposition 3.10)" on pp. 3–4 names the right three statements. I suggest a two-line corollary after Lemma 3.8 stating the bijection explicitly (suggestion S3 below).
- *Uniqueness.* The clause of Theorem 3.6 is stronger and more explicit than the line paper's "unique up to the normalization χ(1) = 1" and is proved independently on p. 37. The headline phrase "up to a relabelling of the scales" is exactly this clause.
- *On operators.* Corollary 3.7 adds Lemma 3.3 in both directions and the uniqueness of the measures; proved on p. 37. The "Scope" sentence (p. 3) "the two forms describe the same families (Lemma 3.3)" is right.

**Where a headline sentence is looser than the result.**

- p. 29, §4, second sentence: "Its main result is that their kernels are the rotation-invariant self-decomposable laws." The stages μ_{s,t} with s > 0 are residuals of a self-decomposable law and are in general *not* self-decomposable (a residual of the Matérn law is not even infinitely divisible in general — the transform ((1+s²ω²)/(1+t²ω²))^γ is that of a stage, and the paper itself says on p. 30 that the stages are not examined). The following sentence is precise ("the kernels from scale zero"). The first sentence should say "kernels from scale zero".
- Abstract, sentence 4 ("The kernels that take the signal to each scale are the dilations of one rotation-invariant self-decomposable law"): precise, since "take the signal to a scale" means μ_{0,t}. The relabelling is not mentioned in the abstract, but "the dilations of one law" is true in every gauge (only the index changes), so no qualification is missing.
- p. 2, line 22: "Every symmetric self-decomposable law other than the point mass occurs" is said for the line and is correct there; the R^d form (rotation-invariant) follows on p. 3.
- p. 3, "The reason is a reduction to the line. A rotation-invariant kernel is determined by its projection on a line through the origin, and the projections of an isotropic family form a family on the line (Lemmas 3.4 and 3.5)." Correct.

**Comparison with the semigroup axioms** (p. 1, p. 8 after Definition 2.1, p. 4 "Relation to scale-space theory"). For my part I only checked that the hemigroup axiom (A6) and the covariance (A8) with free S_λ are stated as in the line paper (they are, verbatim, Definition 2.1 = [9, Def. 3.1], p. 17 of `line-paper.pdf`) and that the sentence "A semigroup asks more than a cascade needs: every stage has to be itself one of the apertures" is accurate for Pauwels et al.'s recursivity K_sK_t = K_{s+t}. No objection.

**"The paper claims no new result for probability theory"** (p. 5). For the statements of my part this is honest: the Sources row for Theorem 3.6 (p. 32) attributes the law-level classification to Sato's Theorem 16.1 (self-similar additive processes; this I could confirm through the citing literature, the book itself not being reachable) and to Hazod–Siebert, Thm. 2.14.21 (hemigroups mapped into themselves by a contracting one-parameter group of automorphisms), which I could not check; the author should quote it. The paper's own contribution at this level is the free gauge (Theorem 3.6 recovers Sato's prescribed action t ↦ t^H as the case χ(t) = t^H and adds the uniqueness of the gauge) and the operator form; that is said correctly on p. 4 ("In both the action of a dilation on the parameter is prescribed. Here it is a relabelling of the scales that the axioms leave free"). Proposition 3.10 is Sato's polar criterion C2 specialised to rotation invariance and is presented as such. Nothing of this part is presented as new that is known.
