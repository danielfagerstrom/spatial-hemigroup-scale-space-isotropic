## 2. The headline claims against what is proved (part C)

The claims about the centre in the abstract (p. 1), the second answer and the first contribution of section 1 (pp. 3–4) and the conclusion (p. 30) are carried by Theorem 3.12, Corollary 3.13, Propositions 3.15–3.17 and A.4–A.6 with the qualifications they carry in the body. Specifically:

- "A density bounded near its centre exactly when c > d" (abstract; p. 3; p. 30) is Theorem 3.12(5), stated for a datum with Gaussian coefficient 0 and c > 0. The qualification c > 0 is not a restriction: since k_d is nonincreasing, c = k_d(0+) = sup k_d, so c = 0 is the trivial datum, excluded by Proposition 3.10(3). The text could say so in one clause (suggestion S4).
- "Otherwise a point source is recorded as a spike, whose growth is computed from the Lévy measure, with its constant" (abstract) is Proposition 3.17: for c < d the rate Q s^{c−d} K(s) with Q in closed form; at c = d the rate B·𝓛(s) with B in closed form and 𝓛 ≥ log(1/s). The headline is right. The reader should know that at c = d the "constant" is the coefficient of 𝓛(s), which is a logarithm only when ∫_0^1 (d − k_d(u)) du/u < ∞ (Proposition 3.17(3)); the introduction (p. 3, "when c = d at least like log(1/|x|)") says this correctly.
- "The number c belongs to the exponent. It is the same in every dimension that admits the exponent" (p. 3) is Corollary 3.13(3), and the inference "a kernel bounded on the line can have a spike in the plane" is clause (5) of the theorem in each dimension with the one c; the Matérn case ½ < γ ≤ 1 (abstract) follows from c = 2γ (Example 3.14) and the threshold γ > d/2. Correct.
- "For c > d + N the density is sure to have N continuous derivatives" (p. 3; p. 30) is Theorem 3.12(2). The ladder is sharp for the Matérn family: at γ = 1/2 on the line (c = 1 = d + 0) the kernel is e^{−|x|}-type, continuous and not C¹, as the theorem allows and no more.

Sentences that read a statement for imaging:

- p. 19, "It costs a user two things. A sampled kernel has no central value. And the peak of the record of a point source depends on the size of the cell that records it: the peak is the mass that the kernel gives the cell divided by the volume of the cell, and it grows without bound as the cell shrinks. The record of a bounded image f stays bounded by sup|f| whatever the kernel." All four sentences are correct readings of Theorem 3.12(4) and of Proposition 3.17 (the mass of a ball of radius s divided by its volume is asymptotic to (d/c) Q s^{c−d} K(s), or B 𝓛(s), p. 24). The last sentence is the right caution against over-reading the spike, and it is true for every probability kernel.
- p. 21, "N continuous derivatives are enough for the derivative filters of order up to N built from the kernel to be continuous functions." True, and what C1 gives is slightly more: the derivatives tend to 0 at infinity, so the derivative filters are bounded continuous functions. What C1 does *not* give is their integrability, which is what makes a derivative filter a bounded operator on L¹ or L^∞; the sentence does not claim that, but a reader in image analysis may take "derivative filter" to include it (suggestion S5).
- p. 24, "The limits for the mass of a ball give the peak of the opening." The word "opening" is not defined anywhere in the paper (it is not in Table 1); from p. 19 the intended object is the record of a point source through a cell of radius s. Required change R2.
- p. 25, the dependence on scale: "For c < d this is asymptotic at the centre to χ(t)^{−c} Q |x|^{c−d} K(|x|)" is correct (K(λx)/K(x) → 1 is Proposition A.6(1)), and the remark that "the radius 1 in K and in C_k is the unit of length at the scale where χ(t) = 1" is the right warning: both constants depend on the choice of gauge normalisation.

Relation to probability (p. 4, "no new result for probability theory"): for the part, the claim is honest and the Sources rows (pp. 32–33) are specific about which case is in print. Two things are presented as the paper's own that deserve one more sentence of attribution: (a) the constant B_c of (A.9), equivalently I_c/Γ(1 + c/2) = 2^{−c} Γ((d−c)/2)/(Γ(d/2) Γ(1 + c/2)), is exactly the constant C̃_{d,α} of Cygan–Grzywny–Trojan [6], Theorem 8 (there for the potential measure of the ball, here for the law at time 1; the Tauberian step is the same), and the Sources row for Proposition 3.16 cites [6] but does not say the constant coincides; (b) Proposition 3.15 is, as the row says, Watanabe's proposition read for a datum; the printed proof via Wolfe's theorem and Blaschke's selection theorem is an alternative route, and the text should say that it is given because the formal development follows it, not because Watanabe's does not cover the case.
