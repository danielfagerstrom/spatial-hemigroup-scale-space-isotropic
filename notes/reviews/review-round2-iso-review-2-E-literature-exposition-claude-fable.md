## 1. Overall assessment and recommendation

**Major revision.** The mathematics I read is, as far as my part goes, consistent and carefully attributed on the probability side: Table 2 is honest, often more generous to the literature than it needs to be, and I found no source cited for more than it says. The main result, the classification of the isotropic hemigroups by the rotation-invariant self-decomposable laws, is new to scale-space theory as far as I can find, and the general theorems of §§3.3–3.4 (the threshold $c > d$ for every isotropic self-decomposable law, the rate at fixed time with its constant, the strict filtration, the separating family) are not in print in that form.

Two things keep it from a minor revision. First, the paper positions itself in scale-space theory and then omits the scale-space work that bears most directly on it: the Bessel scale-space of Burgeth, Didas and Weickert (2005), whose kernels *are* the paper's Matérn kernels, whose "scaled Bessel scale-spaces" are their dilations, and which already remarks that the smoothness of these kernels depends on the dimension; the relativistic scale-spaces of the same authors; the Poisson scale-space of Felsberg and Sommer; the two-parameter "pyramidal" architecture of Alvarez, Guichard, Lions and Morel, which is the hemigroup form of the cascade with the free relabelling of the scales; the first-order recursive filters and Tikhonov regularization, which give the Matérn kernels of integer index on the line; and Lindeberg's time-causal scale space, whose vocabulary the paper uses without citing it. The author's own line and cone papers cite most of these; this manuscript, which says it is readable on its own, silently drops them, and several sentences of §1 are wrong about the field as a result (§2 below). Second, the paper is 86 pages for a 30-page argument, and its main text asks a reader from imaging to absorb two pages of trust-base bookkeeping in the introduction, three pages of cited facts, two restatements of the same axioms, a theorem with five clauses, two versions of the rate, a worked example in four pieces and a notation table of forty rows that exists because sixteen letters mean two things. A journal version should be about 40 pages with the printed proofs of the machine-checked statements in a supplement (§7 below gives the cut).

Whether it is a paper for a mathematical-imaging journal: yes, in scope (JMIV has published the α-scale spaces, the monogenic scale-space and Lindeberg's axiomatics), but only after the cut and after the scale-space relations are put right; as it stands it reads as a paper in the theory of self-decomposable laws with an imaging preface, and a probabilist would send it to a probability journal while an imaging referee would ask what the imaging content is beyond the reading of two theorems. The imaging content exists (the threshold against the dimension, the loss of exponents between dimensions, the recursive-filter reading that the paper does not make) and should be made visible.

## 2. The headline claims, as far as they concern the literature

I take only the sentences that make a claim about prior work or present something as the paper's own; parts A–D cover the claims against the proofs.

**(a) "In the scale-space literature isotropy is imposed together with a semigroup in the scale or with a diffusion equation, and the answer is the Gaussian or a kernel with transform $e^{-a|\xi|^p}$" (p. 4, "Relation to scale-space theory"; same claim p. 1, lines 5–7, and p. 2, "they are the laws at which the semigroup axiom systems arrive").** Wrong as stated. It is true of the semigroup axiomatics *with scale invariance* (Pauwels et al., Florack et al., Duits et al.). It is false of the semigroup scale-spaces without scale invariance: the Bessel scale-space of Burgeth, Didas and Weickert (DSSCV 2005, LNCS 3753, pp. 84–95) is the convolution semigroup with transform $(1+4\pi^2|k|^2)^{-t/2}$, $t \geq 0$, in every dimension $n$, with the explicit kernel $G_n(x,t) \propto K_{(n-t)/2}(|x|)/|x|^{(n-t)/2}$; the relativistic scale-spaces (Scale-Space 2005, LNCS 3459, pp. 1–12) are the semigroups with exponent $\sqrt{|\xi|^2+m^2}-m$. Both are isotropic, both are semigroups, neither is Gaussian or stable. The Bessel kernels are exactly the paper's Matérn kernels of index $\gamma = t/2$ (p. 2: transform $(1+|\omega|^2/2)^{-\gamma}$, up to the scaling of the frequency), composed along the index instead of along the dilation; Burgeth et al. §2.4 also define the "scaled Bessel scale-spaces" $G^n_{t,\sigma}(x) = \sigma^n G^n_t(\sigma x)$, which are the paper's dilations $\mu_{0,t}$ at fixed $\gamma$, and they say that the Bessel scale-space "is not scale invariant". The relativistic kernels are Gaussian variance mixtures against an inverse-Gaussian delay and are self-decomposable by Halgreen's theorem [20], so they are members of the paper's class $\mathcal S$ too. The sentence must be rewritten: "with a semigroup *and scale invariance*", and the two families named as members of the class that the hemigroup axioms recover. The cone paper (its §1 and its §5) says all of this correctly; the manuscript lost it.

**(b) "Among those added are the laws with transform $(1+\omega^2/2)^{-\gamma}$ ... This paper calls these laws and their dilations the Matérn kernels" (p. 2), and "By the first answer the class of kernels is then larger: it contains the stable kernels ... and the Matérn kernels" (p. 4).** "Added" relative to the scale-invariant semigroup axiomatics is correct; as a statement about scale-space theory it misleads, since the kernels are in the field under the name Bessel scale-space (and, on the line, as the $n$-fold cascade of symmetric first-order recursive filters, see (f)). The fix is one sentence of credit and the name: call them the Matérn (Bessel) kernels, and say that what the hemigroup adds is the stage between two dilations at fixed index.

**(c) The dimension effect for the Matérn kernels (abstract; p. 3; p. 22, Example 3.14; p. 30).** The abstract's "a kernel bounded on the line can have a spike in the plane: those with transform $(1+|\xi|^2/2)^{-\gamma}$ ... do so for $1/2 < \gamma \leq 1$" is, for this family, the classical behaviour of the Bessel kernel $G_\alpha$ of Aronszajn and Smith [1], (4,2), which the paper cites on p. 23, and it is remarked inside scale-space theory by Burgeth et al. (p. 87 of their paper): $G_n(x,n+1) \propto e^{-|x|}$ is "continuous, not differentiable at $x=0$", $G_n(x,n+3) \propto (1+|x|)e^{-|x|}$ is $C^2$, and "a merely continuous function convolved with $G_n(x,n+k)$ produces only a $C^{k-1}$-smoothed version", with the dimension $n$ explicit. That is Theorem 3.12(2)'s ladder for the Matérn example, in print in the field in 2005. The paper's contribution is the theorem for every isotropic self-decomposable law with $c$ read off the Lévy measure, not the Matérn instance; the abstract and p. 3 should say so, and Example 3.14 should cite Burgeth et al. beside [1].

**(d) "For probability theory the paper claims no new result" (p. 5) and the two descriptions presented as the paper's own (p. 4, items 1 and 2).** I checked the three things the brief names.

- *The growth of the density at the centre computed from the Lévy measure with its constant, in every dimension (Propositions 3.16, 3.17).* On the line this is Sato and Yamazato [39], as Table 2 says. In $\mathbb R^d$ the nearest statement in print is Grzywny, Ryznar and Trojan [18], Theorems 3.4 and 3.9 (arXiv numbering), for isotropic unimodal semigroups whose exponent is in de Haan's class $\Pi_\ell$, which contains the paper's $F(r) \sim c \log r$ with $\ell \equiv c$: for bounded $\ell$ and $t \to 0$ with $t\psi(1/|x|) \to \infty$, $p(t,x) \sim t\,|x|^{-d}\,\ell(1/|x|)\,e^{-t\psi(1/|x|)}\,\Gamma(d/2)/(2\pi^{d/2})$. That is the paper's rate $Q\,s^{c-d}K(s)$ in the small-time regime: with $\psi(1/s) = c\log(1/s) + \text{const}$ it gives $t c\,\Gamma(d/2)(2\pi^{d/2})^{-1} e^{-t\cdot\text{const}} s^{tc-d}$, and the paper's constant $R(d,tc)$ reduces to $tc\,\Gamma(d/2)/(2\pi^{d/2})$ as $t \to 0$ (since $\Gamma(tc/2) \sim 2/(tc)$). So the paper's result is the fixed-time ($t=1$) version, with the Riesz constant and the slowly varying factor $K$ exact, obtained by a different route (Karamata through a Gaussian average, which Table 2 credits to [6] and [29]). It is new as stated, and the claim "no new result for probability" is a modesty the paper can afford, but Table 2's row for Proposition 3.16/3.17 in $\mathbb R^d$ should name [18], Thm. 3.9 as the closest statement in print and say in which regime it holds. The row now cites [18] only for the threshold (Example 3) and for Prop. 5.3. I could not reach [18]'s Example 3 to check the threshold claim attributed to it.
- *The separating family with its critical values and their limit (Definition 3.20, Theorem 3.21).* Not in print beyond the cone paper's numerical report, which the paper says ([10, Lem. 4.12 and Rem. 4.10]). I found nothing comparable: no family of radial Lévy measures followed through the dimensions by the monotonicity of its "descente". The result is the paper's (and the cone paper's) own.
- *The test for the next dimension through the continuity of the profile one dimension down (Proposition 3.19(1), Lemma A.8).* Not in print for Lévy measures as far as I can find. But a geostatistician recognises the operator: the formula $k_d(r) = (|S^{d-1}|/|S^d|)\int_{\mathbb R} k(r\sqrt{1+u^2})(1+u^2)^{-(d+1)/2}\,du$ is Matheron's *descente* (the inverse of the *montée*, the "dimension walk" of Gneiting 2002 and of [7]), applied to the radial Lévy density instead of to a covariance. The paper cites [7] only for radius laws. Cite Matheron (1965, *Les variables régionalisées et leur estimation*) or Gneiting (2002, *Compactly supported correlation functions based on dimension walks*, JMVA) at Lemma A.8, and say that the test is the descente applied to the Lévy measure. The paper's "shadow" is a new word for an old operator; it should at least be introduced as such.

Nothing else in the main text reads as the paper's own that is standard. Three items a probabilist would want credited more directly: (i) that a self-decomposable law on $\mathbb R^d$ that is not concentrated on a hyperplane has a density is Sato (1982), *Absolute continuity of multivariate distributions of class L*, J. Multivariate Anal. 12, 89–94 (the paper obtains the density for the rotation-invariant case from Wolfe's theorem; Sato 1982 is the standard citation and should be in Table 2's row for Proposition 3.15); (ii) the polar criterion C2 in $\mathbb R^d$ is Sato (1980), *Class L of multivariate distributions and its subclasses*, J. Multivariate Anal. 10, 207–232, cited now only through [38], p. 117; (iii) the variance-gamma law is Madan and Seneta (1990, J. Business 63, 511–524), cited now only through the 2025 review [14]. And the Matérn family in $d$ dimensions with spectral density $(1+|\xi|^2)^{-\gamma}$ is usually credited to Whittle (1954; 1963, *Stochastic processes in several dimensions*), which [19] discusses; the paper cites Matérn's gamma mixture and Stein but not Whittle. The sentence "Matérn obtained it as a gamma mixture of Gaussians ([33], (2.4.5)–(2.4.7))" I could not check.

**(e) Koenderink, Florack et al., Lindeberg, Pauwels et al., Duits et al. (p. 4).** Koenderink [28]: accurate (causality, homogeneity and isotropy give the heat equation; p. 365 plausible). Florack et al. [16]: accurate; I read the paper: (23) is $\hat g(\omega;\sigma) = \exp(\alpha\sigma^p\omega^p)$ and separability fixes $p = 2$ (their p. 380), as the manuscript says; the manuscript's "$\alpha<0$, $p>0$" matches. Florack's book [15]: section and result numbers not checked. Lindeberg [32]: the claim (isotropic case is the Gaussian under non-enhancement with a semigroup) is right; §3.1 and §4.1.1 not checked. Pauwels et al. [35]: accurate in substance (recursivity = semigroup, scale invariance, the class $e^{-a|\omega|^\alpha}$, the plane by rotation invariance); pages not checked. Duits et al. [8]: accurate (generator $-(-\Delta)^\alpha$, positivity for $\alpha \in (0,1]$); pages not checked. The paragraph is accurate about the five it names and wrong about the field by what it leaves out, item (a) above and §6.

**(f) The reading for imaging (pp. 19, 21, 24, 30).** The sentences are sound but thin; see §7. One claim needs a source: "N continuous derivatives are enough for the derivative filters of order up to N built from the kernel to be continuous functions" (p. 21) is fine; the natural companion, which the paper does not make, is that for the Matérn kernels the derivative filters exist only up to order $< 2\gamma - d$, which Burgeth et al. say in their Sobolev form ($R^n_t: H^s \to H^{s+t}$).

## 3. Mathematical correctness

Not my part. In the main-text statements I noticed nothing wrong. Two remarks for the other referees: Proposition 3.15 is proved from Wolfe's theorem (C5) where Watanabe [50] gives the radially nonincreasing density directly from the Lévy measure (Table 2 says so); the detour through C5 costs the formal development an assumption that Watanabe's proposition would replace, and costs the reader the mixing-measure discussion of p. 12. ### 6.2 Scale-space literature that a referee of the field expects and does not find

Everything below is absent from the manuscript's reference list. Items marked † are cited in the author's line paper or cone paper, which makes their absence here a regression rather than an oversight.

1. **Burgeth, Didas, Weickert, *The Bessel scale-space* (DSSCV 2005)†** and ***Relativistic scale-spaces* (Scale-Space 2005)†**: the Matérn kernels in the field, with the scaled version and the dimension-dependent regularity (§2(a)–(c)). Required, in §1 "Relation to scale-space theory", in §2.4 where the Matérn family is named, and in Example 3.14.
2. **Alvarez, Guichard, Lions, Morel (1993)†**, *Axioms and fundamental equations of image processing*. Their architecture is a family $T_t$ with transition operators $T_{t+h,t}$, $T_{t+h} = T_{t+h,t}T_t$ (the "pyramidal structure"/recursivity), and their scale invariance is $T_t H_\lambda = H_\lambda T_{t'(t,\lambda)}$ with a free $t'$: that is the manuscript's (A6) and (A8), hemigroup and relabelling, twenty years before the hemigroup paper. (I quote the axioms from my reading of the paper and of the Guichard–Morel lecture notes; I could not reach the text online to check the wording, and the author should.) What makes their linear case the heat equation is the locality/regularity axiom on $T_{t+h,t}$, exactly what the manuscript does not impose ("Nothing is claimed on locality", p. 4). The sentence on p. 4, "Here neither a semigroup, nor separability, nor a differential equation is assumed", should add that the two-scale architecture is that of Alvarez et al. and that what is dropped relative to them is locality. The line paper has this reference and dismisses it as "orthogonal"; it is not orthogonal, it is the closest architecture in print.
3. **Felsberg and Sommer (2001, DAGM; 2004, JMIV 21)†**, the Poisson scale-space: the manuscript names the Poisson kernel twice (pp. 9, 22) and credits it only to Duits et al. The first scale-space use is Felsberg and Sommer's, and their observation that the Poisson kernel satisfies Iijima's conditions is the one the line paper quotes.
4. **Iijima (1962)† and Weickert, Ishikawa, Imiya (1999, JMIV 10)†**, the first axiomatics; Witkin (1983), Babaud et al. (1986)†, Yuille and Poggio (1986)†: the paper's "the axiom systems of the literature then arrive at the Gaussian" (p. 1) cites none of the uniqueness results it refers to. One sentence with these citations.
5. **Lindeberg and Fagerström (1996, ECCV)†, Lindeberg (2016, JMIV 55; 2023, Biol. Cybern. 117)†, Koenderink (1988, *Scale-time*)†, and the author's own Fagerström (2005, *Temporal scale spaces*, IJCV 64)**: "In time-causal scale space a kernel is the law of a random delay" (pp. 3, 9) uses Lindeberg's term and cites only [11]. The time-causal limit kernel of Lindeberg (2016) is, as the cone paper says, the nearest construction to the subordinated families; the manuscript, which builds its $\mathcal S$ on the time-causal families, should say where time-causal scale space comes from.
6. **Nielsen, Florack, Deriche (1997, JMIV 7)†** and **Deriche (1987/1993)†, Young and van Vliet (1995)†, van Vliet, Young, Verbeek (1998)†**: on the line the Matérn kernel of index 1 is the two-sided exponential, the symmetric first-order recursive filter, and the index-$n$ kernel is the cascade of $n$ such filters with transform $(1+\tau^2\omega^2)^{-n}$; first-order Tikhonov regularization gives the same transform $(1+\lambda\omega^2)^{-1}$ (Nielsen et al.; in the plane its kernel is $K_0$, the Matérn kernel of index 1). The cone paper devotes a section to this; the manuscript, whose headline example is this family, does not mention that it is the family of the recursive Gaussian approximations. This is the imaging reading a JMIV reader wants (§7).
7. **Lindeberg (1990, PAMI 12; 1994, book)†**, discrete scale-space: the paper says "no discretization" (p. 4); a sentence in §4's limitations saying that the discrete counterpart of self-decomposability is open, against Lindeberg's discrete theory (where the kernels are characterised through generating functions and infinite divisibility enters as the semigroup), is expected.
8. **Pseudo-differential and fractional scale spaces**: Duits et al. [8] is cited; Didas, Burgeth, Imiya, Weickert (2005, *Regularity and scale-space properties of fractional high order linear filtering*, SSVM) and Schmidt and Weickert (2016, JMIV, morphological counterparts of linear shift-invariant scale-spaces, which also lists the known linear ones) complete the picture of what the field already has beyond the Gaussian. Optional.
9. **Lévy processes and infinitely divisible laws already in image analysis**: Unser and Tafti (2014, *An Introduction to Sparse Stochastic Processes*, CUP), where Lévy innovations, variance-gamma and Student-$t$ laws are the image model; Grenander and Srivastava (2001, PAMI 23, *Probability models for clutter in natural images*), where the variance-gamma laws appear as "Bessel K forms"†; Wainwright and Simoncelli (1999/2000, NIPS 12), where the paper's "Gaussian variance mixtures" are the "Gaussian scale mixtures" of natural-image statistics. The paper introduces the Lévy vocabulary to an imaging reader as if it were foreign to the field; two sentences would place it. Optional but valuable.
10. **Self-decomposability, class L, hemigroups in scale space.** I searched for "self-decomposable", "class L", "hemigroup" together with scale space / image analysis / image processing and found no prior use other than the author's series. Hazod–Siebert [21] is the right source for hemigroups in probability. The main result as framed for scale-space theory (the classification of the isotropic two-scale cascades with free relabelling by the rotation-invariant self-decomposable laws) is, as far as I can find, new to the field, with the caveat of item 2: the architecture is Alvarez et al.'s and the conclusion in the non-local linear case was not drawn by them.

### 6.3 Self-citation

The classification rests on Theorem 2.3 = [9, Thm. 7.3], and the filtration on [10, Prop. 4.9]; both are Zenodo preprints, version 0.1, with no refereed counterpart, and the time-causal classification [11] likewise. The paper is honest about this (p. 7, "cited in their released version 0.1"; p. 6, "The statements of §2"). A journal will ask what happens to this paper if [9] does not survive review; the answer the paper has (the formal development reproves what it needs from the seven cited facts) should be stated once, plainly, in §2's opening paragraph rather than inferred from §1.1.

## 7. Exposition and structure

The reader I hold the paper to: a researcher in scale-space theory or image analysis who knows Fourier analysis and basic probability, has not read the earlier papers, and does not know Lévy processes or regular variation.

### 7.1 Does that reader know, after the abstract and page 1, what the paper shows and why it matters?

Mostly yes for *what*, partly for *why*. The abstract states the result, the threshold, the dimension effect and the filtration in nine sentences, which is good. Three faults, each with a fix.

- Abstract, sentences 8–9: "One F gives a kernel in each dimension that admits it", "Each higher dimension admits strictly fewer F": "admit" is undefined and the reader cannot guess that a dimension can refuse an exponent. Fix: "A function F that is the exponent of a kernel on the line need not be one in the plane: each higher dimension admits strictly fewer F, ..."
- Abstract, last sentence: "except four proved in print: this correspondence with its readings, that family as laws, and a constant's closed form" — "this correspondence" and "that family" point back across three sentences and the reader cannot resolve them. Fix: drop the list from the abstract ("except four, listed in §1.1").
- Page 1 says what is at stake ("a kernel may record an ideal point source as a spike ... and a family of the line need not exist in the plane") but not why a user would want the larger class. The one-sentence answer the paper has (p. 2: the Matérn kernels, with their recursive implementation and their covariance reading) is buried; put it on p. 1: "the class gains, among others, the Matérn (Bessel) kernels, which on the line are the cascades of first-order recursive filters."

### 7.2 Where the main text loses the reader

- **p. 2, "The cascade makes that law self-decomposable ... Such a law is the convolution of a centred Gaussian ... with the law of a sum of independent displacements. ... the density of that measure at the size x, multiplied by x, is the profile of the law."** Good prose, but "profile" here is the folded profile of the line, and p. 17 introduces a different "polar profile" ($|S^{d-1}|r^d f_d(r)$, not $x f(x)$) under the same word, and then p. 18 says "the polar profile is not the folded profile of the same exponent". The reader who trusted p. 2 is now lost. Fix: say "on the line" on p. 2 and announce that $\mathbb R^d$ has its own profile.
- **pp. 5–7, §1.1 "What is machine-checked".** Two pages of trust-base bookkeeping ("The form of five formal statements", "The review of the formal statements") before the mathematics starts. Essential to the paper's claims, wrong in this place. Fix: keep six lines in §1 (what is checked, what is proved in print, what is computed, pointer to Appendix B) and move the rest to Appendix B.
- **pp. 7–8 and p. 14: the axioms twice.** Definition 2.1 (the line) and Definition 3.1 ($\mathbb R^d$) are the same list with $\mathbb R$ replaced by $\mathbb R^d$ and the reflection by $O(d)$, and Remark 3.22 says so. Fix: state Definition 3.1 once for $d \geq 1$ in §2 or §3.1, say that at $d = 1$ it is [9, Def. 3.1], and delete Definition 2.1 and the comments after it (the comment on (A6) vs Pauwels, p. 8, moves to §3.1).
- **pp. 11–13, §2.6, the cited facts in full with their departures from the sources.** Three pages that the imaging reader cannot use before seeing where the facts enter, and that interrupt the path from the axioms to Theorem 3.6. Fix: one paragraph in §2 naming C1–C7 with one line each; the statements and the discussion of departures go to Appendix B next to Table 3, where the trust-base reader needs them.
- **Terms used before they are defined** (page of first use → page of definition): "slowly varying" (p. 3, "a factor that varies more slowly than every power" → p. 13); "Lévy process" (p. 9, "an increasing Lévy process" → p. 32, in the Sources, "a process with stationary independent increments"), define at p. 9; "heat kernel $g_t$ of Lemma A.2" (p. 21, in Theorem 3.12(4), defined in an appendix lemma; the formula is given two paragraphs later), define in the theorem; "$C_k$, $K$ and $\varkappa_d$ as in Proposition A.6" (p. 24, Proposition 3.17's statement depends on an appendix proposition for its constants although p. 23–24 displays them), make a Definition in §3.3; "the opening" / "the peak of the opening" (p. 24, "The limits for the mass of a ball give the peak of the opening") — never defined anywhere; I think "aperture" or "the record of a point source" is meant; "window by window" (p. 29, "the strictness of the filtration window by window") is jargon for "between each pair of consecutive dimensions"; "scaled inverse-gamma law of shape $\vartheta$ of [10, Prop. 5.10]" (p. 19) depends on the cone paper for a name though the density is given; "de Haan's theorem" (p. 32) fine in the Sources.
- **Theorem 3.12 (pp. 20–21), five clauses, one page.** Clause (3) is the subordinated-slice special case with the mixture formula and the value at the centre; clause (4) carries the quantitative heat-kernel bounds. For the reader the theorem is (1), (2), (5); (3) is an addition for the Matérn example and (4) is the proof's quantitative residue. Fix: state Theorem 3.12 as (1), (2), (5); make (3) a proposition in §3.3 before Example 3.14; move (4)'s quantitative bounds to Appendix A beside A.4/A.5 (keep the first sentence of (4), the divergence, in the theorem).
- **Propositions 3.16 and 3.17 (pp. 22–24): the rate twice.** 3.16 is through the transform, 3.17 through the profile, with the same density $q$ and the same limits. The reader wants one: the profile version, which is what "the designer specifies" (p. 23), with the transform version as its lemma in the appendix. The paragraph on p. 22–23 that explains regular variation and the Riesz kernel is good and should stay.
- **Example 3.14 in four pieces (pp. 22, 23, 25, 27).** Each "continued" piece requires the reader to recover $\gamma$, $d$, $c = 2\gamma$ and the closed form from pages earlier. Fix: one example, after Proposition 3.17, with a five-line table: $c = 2\gamma$; bounded iff $\gamma > d/2$; $p(x) \sim$ const $|x|^{2\gamma-d}$ for $\gamma < d/2$ with the constant; $\sim$ const $\log(1/|x|)$ at $\gamma = d/2$; $C_k, K(0+), A, Q$ for $\gamma = 3/4$, $d = 2$; $\phi(r) = (1+r^2/2)^{-1}$ in the dictionary. The p. 27 piece (the Matérn exponent through the dictionary) can stay in §3.4 with a back-reference.
- **Proposition 3.18 (pp. 26–27).** A page-long statement in which the reader meets $\Omega_d$, $\phi$, $X$, $R$, $U$, $\log^+$, two moment conditions and three clauses. The introduction before it (p. 26) is good. Fix: state (1)–(2) as "the pairs $(c, X)$ and the exponents of logarithmic growth are in bijection, with $k_d = c\Pr(|X| > \cdot)$ and $\phi(r) = 1 - rF'(r)/c$", and (3) as the test; the two technical conditions and the formula for $\phi$ in terms of $\Omega_d$ go in a remark.
- **Notation collisions (Table 1, pp. 31–32, "Sixteen letters keep a second role").** The table is the symptom; the fix is to rename. The collisions that bite in the main text: $c$ is the profile at the origin throughout and the ratio of gauges in the uniqueness clause of Theorem 3.6 (p. 16), the main theorem; $K$ is the slowly varying factor of Proposition 3.17 and $K_\nu$ the Bessel function on the same page (p. 25: "$K_{d/2}(\sqrt 2\,r)$ ... the factor $K$ is bounded"); $Q$ is an orthogonal matrix in Definition 3.1 and Proposition 3.18 and the constant of Proposition 3.17 in between; $A$ is the constant of Proposition 3.17, the constant of Proposition A.5 and the Gaussian matrix of C2–C3; $R$ is the reflection, the Riesz constant $R(d,c)$ and a random radius. Rename the constants ($\varrho$ for the gauge ratio, $\mathsf K$ or $\Lambda$ for the slow factor, $C_Q, C_A$ or $Q_{d,c}$ for the constants) and Table 1 shrinks to the symbols the reader needs.
- **§1 says the answers three times** ("The answers", pp. 3–4; "What the paper contributes", pp. 4–5; §4, pp. 29–30, which repeats "The answers" nearly verbatim). Fix: merge "The answers" and "What the paper contributes" into one; make §4 the limitations and the open problems only (one paragraph of conclusion).
- **Readable on its own?** Yes for the statements, with the exceptions above (Prop. 3.11(4), "canonical gauge" and "delay law" by [10, Rem. 4.3], Prop. 2.7 used only in discussion). The restated statements carry their released numbers, which is good practice.

### 7.6 Is it a paper for a mathematical-imaging journal?

In scope, yes; as written, no. What an imaging reader takes from the paper is three sentences (the class is the rotation-invariant self-decomposable laws; a kernel with $c \leq d$ spikes; the plane admits fewer kernels than the line), and the 80 pages between them are Lévy measures, Tauberian theorems and Lean inventories. What would make it a JMIV paper: the cut of §7.5; the scale-space relations put right (§6.2, items 1–6), which also supplies the imaging reading the paper lacks — that the Matérn kernels are the Bessel scale-space kernels and the recursive-filter cascades, that the dimension effect was first seen there, and that the architecture is Alvarez et al.'s without locality; and one worked case in imaging terms: the Matérn kernel of index 1 in the plane ($K_0$, the first-order Tikhonov filter, $c = 2 = d$: logarithmic spike) against index 1 on the line (bounded, the two-sided exponential). If the author will not cut the proofs, the paper is a probability paper and should say so and go to a probability or analysis journal, where "no new result for probability" would then have to be withdrawn, because there are new results (§2(d)).
